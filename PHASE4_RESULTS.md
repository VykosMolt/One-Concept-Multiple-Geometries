# PHASE4_RESULTS.md

**SAMPLE-LIMITED ONLY — the frozen-transient hypothesis fails, in three independent ways:** (1) equivalence error of a
sparse source alias is a smooth power law in cumulative alias exposures (latent JS ≈ 1.2·N^−0.74), identical across
rarity levels at matched exposure and never frozen; (2) a line-aligned output code *accelerates* rather than delays
equivalence; (3) an equally rare unique state is learned about as well as the rare alias — the model does not exploit
the equivalence at all (RARITY, NOT SYMMETRY), and its representation collapses *before* its behaviour equalizes.
Separately, the natural premise is false for the rarest natural twin: in Wikipedia the next-key rows of "Cb major" and
"B major" are not the same predictive state (latent JS 0.228, 81st percentile of all pairs).

*(Design frozen in `PHASE4_DESIGN.md`; chronology in `PHASE4_LOG.md`; code `synthetic/phase4.py`, tests
`tests/test_phase4.py` (pass); 91 runs under `results/phase4/runs/`; aggregate tables generated by
`synthetic/phase4_analyze.py` and the exposure-collapse script (`results/phase4/analysis.txt`,
`exposure_collapse.txt`, `corpus_rows.txt`); figure `figures/phase4/curves.png`. Phase III is untouched.)*

## 1. Rarity schedule and design
Same 15 labels / 12 latent classes / three alias pairs / CIRCLE target law (β = 2, balanced target aliases) as Phase III.
Latent classes are sampled uniformly (mass 1/12, constant across r); within each duplicated class the SOURCE label is
the rare alias with probability r ∈ {0.5, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001} and the common alias otherwise; the target
distribution is unchanged, so twin oracle rows are identical for every r (unit-tested). Data sampled online; every
checkpoint records steps and the actual cumulative number of rare-alias source draws. Aligned vs permuted codes with
paired seeds. Primary: 6 r × 2 codes × 5 seeds at 12k steps; extended r ∈ {0.003, 0.001} × 2 × 3 seeds at 48k steps;
unique-state control (class of label 0 made rare at r, aliases balanced): 3 r × 2 × 3 seeds.

## 2. Natural-frequency calibration
Wikipedia major-key mentions: Cb:B = 38:1544 (r = 0.024), C#:Db = 121:438 (r = 0.22), Gb:F# = 162:232 (r = 0.41);
rare-alias absolute counts 38–162 per 3.1B words; explicit enharmonic co-mention windows 7/18/9. The sweep spans 20×
below and above the natural ratio. Wikipedia is used only to place the regime, not as the models' training set.

## 4–5. Source-twin JS (15-way) and latent-class twin JS (primary)
Tolerance from the balanced runs: 3 × q95 of their final latent JS = 0.00025 (they reach it after 1.2–3.1 × 10⁵ rare
exposures). Final latent JS at 12k steps: r = 0.5: 0.0000; 0.1: 0.0001; 0.03: 0.0002; 0.01: 0.0006 / 0.0011; 0.003:
0.0016 / 0.0033; 0.001: 0.0049 / 0.0063 (aligned / permuted). Extended runs (48k steps): r = 0.003 → 0.0006 / 0.0011 at
9.2k exposures; r = 0.001 → 0.0016 / 0.0023 at 3.1k exposures. No sparse run reaches the strict tolerance within
budget, but every one keeps descending. 15-way JS tracks the latent JS within 20–30% (no separate spelling story).

## 8. Cumulative-exposure scaling (the decisive analysis)
Pooling 1,254 checkpoints from all alias runs: latent JS ≈ 1.18 · exposure^(−0.74) (Spearman −0.86); partial Spearman
of log JS with log exposure given log step = **−0.84**, with log step given log exposure = **−0.19**. Exposure-matched
medians coincide across r (1,600–3,200 exposures: 0.0034 / 0.0032 / 0.0024 / 0.0024 / 0.0025 for r = .1/.03/.01/.003/
.001, aligned; 6,400–12,800: 0.0019 / 0.0009 / 0.0006 / 0.0006). Curves at different r collapse when expressed in rare-
alias exposures (`figures/phase4/curves.png`, middle column). The phenomenon is sample-limited; training history at
other states neither freezes nor materially accelerates it.

## 7. Aligned vs permuted code
Aligned code gives *lower* latent JS at every sparse r (final r = .01: 0.0006 vs 0.0011, 0/5 seeds positive, t p = .020;
r = .003: 0.0016 vs 0.0033; r = .001: 0.0049 vs 0.0063, p = .035; power-law prefactor 0.94 vs 1.5 at the same exponent)
and smaller twin distances at `<Q>` (0.16–0.28 vs 0.25–0.44 of the non-twin distance). As in Phase III, shared tokens
between neighbouring codewords let the rare alias borrow from its neighbours' targets. "Code delays equivalence" is
rejected; the code helps.

## 6. Representation collapse
`<Q>` twin distance falls below 0.30 of the mean non-twin distance after ≈ 250–2,000 rare exposures (aligned) /
≈ 440–2,400 (permuted), whereas behavioural latent JS < 0.002 requires ≈ 1.5k–7.7k exposures; in every run where both
thresholds are crossed, the representational one comes first (e.g. r = .01 aligned: rep 963–1,930, beh 2,485–7,698).
Representation leads behaviour — the opposite of a representational residue lagging correct behaviour.

## 9. Equally-rare unique-state control
At matched exposure the rare unique state's row KL is comparable to (aligned) or better than (permuted) the rare
alias's: r = .01 unique 0.0064 (2.6k exposures) vs alias 0.0105 (2.3k, r = .003); permuted 0.0129 vs 0.0158;
r = .001: 0.037 vs 0.027 at 253 vs 756 exposures. Learning that alias A has the same predictive state as alias B costs
about the same evidence as learning a new state from scratch: nothing in the model transfers the twin's row to the
rare alias. RARITY, NOT SYMMETRY.

## 10. Is the natural Phase-II line now plausibly explained?
Not by a frozen transient. Two things follow instead:
(a) With the synthetic exponent, ≈ 10⁴ rare exposures give latent JS ≈ 10⁻³; even Wikipedia-scale counts (38–162
mentions of the rare spellings) multiplied by any plausible pretraining-corpus factor put the natural models far past
the regime where a sparse alias remains inequivalent — yet OLMo-1B/Gemma/Qwen keep enharmonic twins behaviourally far
apart (Phase II ECI 0.7–0.9). Sparse exposure cannot be the reason.
(b) The premise itself fails in the data: Wikipedia's conditional next-key rows for Cb major and B major differ
(latent JS 0.228; Cb → B/Cb .37, Gb/F# .22, Eb .11; B → D .15, E .14, C .14, F .13); Gb/F# (0.050) and Db/C# (0.068)
are moderately similar. The row-divergence matrix over the 15 spellings correlates with the line (raw RSA +0.53)
more than with the circle (+0.33); under the orthographic controls used throughout (glyph class, edit distance,
letter, alphabet, commonness) the partial reverses (circle +0.60, line +0.05) because in text *key-signature side and
spelling class are the same variable*. The models' next-key behaviour matches these corpus rows (RSA +0.44 OLMo-1B,
+0.24 Gemma, +0.26 OLMo-7B, +0.12 Qwen). So the natural "line" is, at least for the sparse spellings, a faithful
reflection of spelling-class-conditioned corpus statistics — Phase II's "no task-conditioned line" held only under a
convention that books spelling class as orthography rather than as the tonal-pitch-class coordinate (Temperley/Moss).
Which convention is right is a music-theoretic, not a statistical, question; the data do not decide it.

## 11. Strongest counter-evidence
- The strict tolerance (0.00025) was never reached by sparse runs within 48k steps; a reader could call that
  "unresolved". The exposure-collapse and the unique-state control argue against any special mechanism, but a much
  longer budget at r = 0.001 was not run.
- Rare-row KL and global KL differ by 3–20×: the "aggregate metrics hide unresolved quotient structure" pattern is
  real in magnitude terms, just not frozen.
- The natural corpus rows for Gb/F# and Db/C# *are* moderately equivalent, so the "premise fails" argument is strongest
  for Cb/B only.
- One model size, one code family, online sampling (no repeated-example memorization dynamics); no target-rarity or
  2×2 rarity experiments were run (stop condition).
- The corpus RSA with model behaviour (+0.12…+0.44) is modest; the models are not simply copying Wikipedia rows.

## 12. Technical note or paper?
The four phases now form a closed argument: corpus co-occurrence is periodic under the standard controls; LLM key-name
geometry is orthographic; next-key behaviour follows the spelled line; output codes can only tilt representations and
transiently bias learning; sparse-alias equivalence is ordinary sample complexity, not a frozen transient; and the
natural line at the seam is at least partly present in spelling-conditioned corpus statistics. That is a coherent
short paper about *what statistic an LLM's key geometry reflects and why the standard instruments misread it* — with
the honest caveat that "line vs circle in the data" hinges on whether spelling class is counted as orthography or as
tonal structure. It is not a paper about a new learning phenomenon.

## 13. Single most informative next experiment
A targeted natural-model test of the spelling-conditioned reading: build the conditional next-key matrix from a much
larger corpus slice (OLMo-mix wiki + DCLM sample) separately for spelled contexts (flat-side vs sharp-side prose) and
check whether each model's behavioural rows track the corpus rows better than any theory geometry does — i.e., replace
"circle vs line" with "does the model reproduce the conditional statistic of spelled text", which Phase IV suggests is
the question that actually has a yes/no answer.
